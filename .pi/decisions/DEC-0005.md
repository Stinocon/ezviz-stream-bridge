# DEC-0005 — the pump never parks writing FFmpeg, whichever input it is feeding

## Statement

The frontmatter is the invariant. Two parts, and they stand or fall together:

1. **The video stdin is a bounded queue when there is an audio input.** `_VideoPipe` is
   the same shape as `_AudioPipe` (DEC-0004): a non-blocking descriptor, chunks queued
   whole, the oldest dropped past the bound and counted, the session reporting the
   count. The one difference is deliberate: an error on the video write raises, because
   a closed stdin means FFmpeg is gone and the session has to end rather than drain the
   camera into a queue nobody reads. The audio pipe swallows its write errors for the
   same reason in reverse: a dead FFmpeg is the video write's to report.

2. **The audio input runs on its demuxer's clock, placed by a measured offset.** No
   `-use_wallclock_as_timestamps` on the audio: an ADTS stream is paced by its own
   demuxer at a frame per 1024 samples, which is burst-proof, and the read time the flag
   would stamp is exactly what the queue turns into a burst. `-itsoffset` carries the
   gap this session measured between its first audio packet and its first video one,
   which is the whole of the audio's alignment with the video: the offset is constant,
   not a correction that drifts.

## Reason

DEC-0004 left the video pipe out on purpose: "FFmpeg is draining it through its probe,
and a session whose video stops has nothing to protect." That was written from the
single-input picture and it does not survive the two-input one. The interleaver will not
drain the video past the audio's timestamps, so from the first audio packet to the end of
the session the video is read late; the probe's worth of video has to wait somewhere, the
OS pipe is too small for it, and the pump that parks holding it is the pump that was
keeping the audio alive. The lab reproduction parked for 5.42s in one `write`; the
reporter's log shows the same cycle ending at +8.542s with 41 of 288 AUs served.

The bound (`_VIDEO_PENDING_BYTES`, 8 MiB) is sized to the backlog it exists to absorb:
one probe's worth of a camera's bitrate, which is seconds of video at hundreds of KiB/s.
A consumer further behind than that is not slow but stalled, and the degradation is
bounded and visible (the oldest chunks dropped, the count warned about) where the
deadlock's degradation was a mute.

## Context / relations

Extends DEC-0004's invariant from the audio pipe to both of FFmpeg's inputs, for the
sessions that have both; the no-audio session keeps the blocking stdin and its
backpressure on purpose, and DEC-0004's record of that choice stays as it was for that
case. Depends on DEC-0002 (the audio input is decided before FFmpeg starts) and
DEC-0003 (the stall watchdog), which is no longer the only thing standing between a slow
consumer and a mute but remains the answer to a camera that stops sending audio.

## Verification

`pytest -k "starve or over_the_queue_bound"` pins both halves: a jammed stdin must leave
the audio served whole (the 0.1.13 deadlock is a test that fails on the old code), and a
queue past its bound must say what it dropped while the audio keeps flowing. The
`verify_command` above runs them; the addon-image gate covers the argv on the real
FFmpeg.
